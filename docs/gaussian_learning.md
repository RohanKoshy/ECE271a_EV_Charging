# Ideal plant with learned Gaussian demand

Run this alternative after installing the repository's environment:

```sh
python scripts/run_learning_experiment.py
```

The separate runner leaves the original MPC, experiment runner, and configs unchanged.
The default config is `configs/experiments/mixture_learning.yaml`. It samples a
hidden car type for each session from weights [0.4, 0.4, 0.2], then samples
nonnegative Gaussian demand for that type: (mean, sd) = (8, 2), (20, 3), or
(35, 4) kWh. Type labels and true parameters are not given to the controller.
It learns three means, variances, and mixture weights from exact and censored
observations. Component estimates are sorted by mean for stable reporting;
these are statistical groups, not identified vehicle makes. Small samples may
not identify all three groups accurately.

To retain the single-Gaussian experiment, pass
`configs/experiments/gaussian_learning.yaml` explicitly.

The console asks for 3, 5, or 10 newly completed sessions between replans.
For noninteractive runs, pass `--replan-every-completions 3` (or 5 or 10).
Outputs are `metrics.csv`, `summary.csv`, and `learning_trace.csv`; there is no plotting.
The trace includes each fitted component mean, variance, and weight.
The default config runs only the learning controller.

## Model

Each component is a Gaussian truncated below zero. In the single-Gaussian configuration,
every session draws from one shared component. `learning.truth.mean` and `learning.truth.sd` define the underlying
Gaussian parameters. They are known only to the generator and the oracle comparator.
The truncated distribution's actual moments differ; both underlying and physical
moments are included in the learning trace. For a mixture, the total mean and variance summarize the component population.

Session windows, site capacity, and charger limits are known. The ideal plant follows
power commands up to physical limits and stops when its private demand is satisfied.
Measurements and completion signals are exact. Source session energy requests are
not used as individual demand forecasts: demand is generated by the shared model.
ACN/CSV sources can supply windows and limits, but this experiment still generates
synthetic demand and does not fit historical ACN energy values.

## Learning and planning

The estimator starts from configurable component parameters and weights. It maximizes
truncation-aware likelihood with a weak configurable prior penalty. Completed
sessions supply exact demands; unfinished sessions supply right-censored lower
bounds. Repeated measurements replace the same session record rather than adding
independent samples. Records persist across days and reset per scenario. Failed
fits retain the previous model and are flagged in the trace.

At replans, the optimizer uses the fitted expected shortfall conditioned
on each unfinished session having needed more than its delivered energy. Mixture
weights are also conditioned on that lower bound before combining shortfalls. Its
convex epigraph uses 65 tangents per unfinished session. The energy cap is the
conditional 99.99th percentile, a numerical planning bound rather than a guarantee.
Completed EVs cannot receive further energy. Past observed charging is fixed.
The remaining configured objective terms and physical constraints are reused.

The alternative supports expected-shortfall mode; it does not implement quantile
mode. In console runs, completion is the event: each session counts once. The
controller solves initially, then at the next slot after at least N new completions.
It does not also solve on the periodic timer. Counts reset each day, and leftover
completions below N do not cause a solve; if no further EVs complete, no further
solve occurs that day. Arrivals and departures are not triggers. This simple policy
can delay corrections when EVs remain unfinished.

Direct API calls retain periodic behavior unless `replan_every_completions` is set.
Learning records update every slot; the estimator fits at replans and day end. No true demand is
revealed to the learner at arrival.

## Comparisons

- `learning_mpc`: estimates mean and variance from observations.
- `fixed_mpc`: holds the initial distribution fixed.
- `oracle_mpc`: knows the generating distribution, but not individual demands.

All methods receive identical sessions, demand realizations, and prices. The oracle
is a distribution-information comparator, not a hindsight optimum or a guaranteed
lower bound on realized cost. Finite samples and censoring mean learning need not
improve every day's performance. The default experiment uses synthetic data only.

Modules: `learning/gaussian.py`, `learning/mixture.py`, `sim/learning_mpc.py`, `sim/learning_runner.py`, and
`sim/plant.py`. The entry point is `scripts/run_learning_experiment.py`.

## Four-policy comparison

```sh
python scripts/compare_controls.py
```

This runs the base settings (100 sessions/day, 5-minute slots, three days, seed 0)
with identical workloads for a fixed day-ahead schedule, hourly MPC, and MPC
triggered by 3 or 5 new completions. Every policy resets to the same mixture
prior each day and enforces the 5 kW safe-power and 150 kW site limits.
Day-ahead solves once and follows its schedule, stopping EVs when full.

The comparison uses an equivalent sparse QP formulation in `sim/compact_solver.py`
that removes unavailable decision slots and introduces explicit energy and fleet
power variables. The objective, constraints, and regularization are preserved.
The original optimizer is unchanged. Outputs in `results/mpc_comparison/`
include four PNG/PDF fleet-load plots, metrics, traces, workload arrays, and the
resolved config. Solve counts include the initial solve; end-of-day fitting is
not counted as a charging optimization. Runtime includes learning and simulation.
